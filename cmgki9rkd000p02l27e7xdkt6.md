There was a point or two recently where I wasn’t sure this path was mine.

I had already pivoted careers. I started in computer science later than most. I had more doubt creeping in than certainty.

And then Vereda happened.

### The Friend Who Wouldn’t Take No for an Answer

Vereda isn’t just an app to me. It’s a reinforced sentiment that all it takes is one idea, one person, one spark to turn a situation around. At a time when I was questioning whether I belonged in this field at all, one of my closest friends refused to let me shrink. He didn’t care about my excuses or how I was feeling in the moment, he wasn’t gonna allow me to doubt myself into oblivion. Instead, he handed me a problem and told me to grow into it. Not because he needed me in order to execute his vision, but more so because he believed that I could and he believed that the power would override any rejection I was feeling in the moment.

### The Engineering That Sparked a Shift

Vereda forced me into truly unfamiliar territory. It was centered around real-time data submission, map rendering, geospatial indexing. User generated content moderation. backend reliability. Frontend responsiveness. It wasn’t in line with the personal projects I had undertook before. It was a mess. It required more complex thought processes than I had been tasked with up to that point. It forced me to consider tradeoffs and required performance awareness. It took into account user behavior and scale. For the first time since I began programming, I wasn’t retroactively solving problems after deliberation and coming to a conclusion with all the info laid out in front of me, instead we were solving problems because users would interact with the result. And that completely changed how I thought.

### The Rebuilding

Maybe if not for Vereda I would have considered another path. But after, it certainly reinforced that this is where I wanted to be. Vereda became the climb that would ascend me further. This one project taught me that confidence is built and not granted. It taught me that sometimes the confidence you seek, can be borrowed from another until you are able to build your own.

Real projects expose weaknesses faster than staying in your comfort zone ever will. And I learned that where I thrive is definitely outside of that comfort zone. This project will forever be tied to the friend who refused to let me fail myself. He saw something and believed in me before I was able to see it in myself. And I will forever repay him by continuing this climb for a lifetime. The fall that preceded was what I needed to stop just dancing with the idea of becoming an engineer and to start committing to it. And once I started that climb, I haven’t stopped climbing since.

Thank you Leo.
