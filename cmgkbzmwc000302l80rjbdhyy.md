### Overview

My professional software engineering journey started at General Motors, where, following my internship, I spent nearly 2 transformative years on the Fleet & Field Applications team. The focus of our team during this time was simple: migrate all customer-facing applications from Oracle WebLogic to Azure Cloud. The monolithic applications were originally designed on WebLogic, but with the shift toward microservices and CI/CD methodologies, we were up against the clock on modernization. The timeframe given was short but attainable: 9 months to rewrite and deploy all Fleet applications on Azure, with additional features planned for future releases. This felt like the equivalent to my first swim test after lessons being jumping off a boat in the middle of Lake Travis, daunting but motivating.

### Team Approach

To solve the problem of completing a complete migration of 5 applications in a little over 9 months, we divided our team and responsibilities in order to simultaneously attack the entire project at once. Our team of 13 developers were split evenly into 3 squads of 4, with one senior engineer floating from team to team depending on developmental needs. The goal was simple, start and finish the migration of your designated application within the given timeframe and, with any leftover bandwidth, assist the other squads if possible. The squad I was assigned to was given the daunting task of migrating the largest application, Medium-Duty Port Inventory or MDPI, which alone accounted for the size of two of the others.

### Responsibilities

For the entirety of my time on this project I worked as a Full-Stack Software Engineer, contributing to every phase of the development lifecycle. I was one of a team of 4 developers responsible for the complete migration of two applications, MDPI and FOS. While my focus occasionally shifted between backend and frontend tasks, I played an integral role across multiple facets of the project. My main responsibilities included:

* Rebuilding the entire MDPI platform, managing its full lifecycle and upgrading the foundational infrastructure
    
* Developing and integrating frontend components aligned with UI/UX requirements
    
* Integrating automated testing pipelines to support continuous workflow deployment
    
* Scheduling automation pipelines in both UI and API for daily execution and monitored test results
    
* Supporting the design and establishment of microservices, contributing to the foundational architecture of the application in ADO.NET

### Company Impact

Commercial fleet sales are a major part of General Motors, with the sector totaling between 15-20% of total revenue for the company annually. Fleet applications extend beyond sales, supporting critical in-house systems, bringing the total revenue responsible to approximately $26 billion. This migration not only stabilized a multitude systems critical to the success of the company but rebuilt the infrastructure that our engineers rely on.
