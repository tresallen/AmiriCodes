Before I ever laid eyes on production code, I worked in a hospital laboratory. My initial schooling was for Forensic Chemistry. My days were completely structured, methodical, precise. Accuracy mattered most. Processes were exact and never changed. The stakes were real and in a lot of cases every single second counted.

But software engineering? That was brand new. I had only been in my computer science curriculum for about a year when I landed my internship at General motors. I understood syntax. I could work through any assignment. I had built small projects. But I had never experienced how a true professional engineer actually thought. I had never seen the process of team members differ so greatly. It was truly opening a can of worms, but in the best way possible.

### Initial Exposure

During my first week with the QA Automation team, I realized rather quickly that writing code in school is nothing close to the same as building systems in production. Engineers don’t just write functions based on the topic presented to them that chapter. They discussed tradeoffs. They debated architecture. They reviewed each other’s PRs line by line, and even if they did not agree 100% on the method, as long as the logic was sound and harmless they agreed to disagree. They thought about maintainability months ahead. Everything was about automation. There was a palpable rhythm to the work. There was a tangible process through it all.  
  
Standup. Sprint planning. Refactoring conversations. Testing discussions. Deployment pipelines. Rinse. Repeat. I had structure in a professional environment before. But I never had it like THIS. It was mind blowing and intriguing all in one.

### Role & Responsibility

I joined the Automation team and began contributing to Java-based test automation frameworks built with Spring Boot. I was paired with a Junior engineer and we were overseen by a senior who was cool as a cucumber. His only goal was for us to continuously find walls to break through. And that we did.

I helped on multiple fronts: Building RESTful test services to replace legacy RobotFramework suites; Develop API-driven automation frameworks; Integrate Elasticsearch test validation; Implement CI/CD pipelines using Azure DevOps; and Automate execution triggers tied to codebase changes. At the time I did not fully understand the extent of what I was doing, but at the end when I had to present all of my work, it clicked. I felt a shift. For the first time, I was contributing to real world systems that would benefit customers daily. It wasn’t just some busy work handed down for the sake of it. I actually had an impact.

### Perspective

Looking back, the biggest impact wasn’t necessarily the work that was completed. In fact, it wasn’t technical at all. It was the shift in perspective. The systems I touched served millions of customers. And that shifted my perspective. At the medical lab, precision meant following the establish protocols and testing procedures laid out for us. And resulting according to the prescribed guidelines. In software engineering, precision meant designing better, and honing better systems overall. Everyday I witness the guys breakdown systems, identify dependencies, and build reusable components. They would always write tests before trusting the code, and they were adamant on automating what should NOT require human effort.

This wasn’t just “working,” they were engineering. And they gave me the confidence to believe that I could do the same.
